This roadmap covers ICPC preparation from preliminaries to World Finals through 56 topics and 198 practice problems. Preliminaries and Regionals account for 36 topics and 142 problems, forming the largest part of the collection.

The topic directory contains the full list of topics and prerequisites. Past papers includes contest sets and selected problem mappings. The training routine covers schedules, team practice, and reference-library preparation.

The past-paper gallery contains 31 full contests, short test sessions, and specialist supplements. Search by name or year and filter by stage, region, or packet type. Solution links and reviewed problem mappings are hidden behind spoiler controls. The website is packaged with index.html at the root and supporting content in src/.

Topic structure

Each topic includes prerequisites, learning objectives, study resources, a mastery check, common pitfalls, and relevant practice problems. The connected diagrams represent dependencies within each stage. Prerequisite IDs also identify dependencies from earlier stages.

Topic IDs are reference labels, not a strict learning order. For example, R05 depends on R06. W01–W06 are specialist electives; they are not all required for World Finals integration.

Practice collection

The collection contains 79 Codeforces, 92 CSES, 17 AtCoder, and 10 Library Checker problems. Past-contest examples are additional to these 198 problems.

Practice roles describe difficulty relative to the topic:

  • Starter: introductory applications and prerequisite drills.
  • Core: the main techniques and reasoning covered by the topic.
  • Stretch: more demanding applications or combinations with other techniques.

Codeforces ratings are reference values and may change. Problems from other platforms are not assigned equivalent numeric ratings. The “why it fits” notes describe a suitable technique and may reveal solution hints.

Scope and assumptions

Stage placement reflects preparation priorities and prerequisites; it is not an official ICPC qualification syllabus. A topic may appear at multiple competition levels. The specialist stage emphasizes selective depth and contest integration rather than a mandatory list of advanced algorithms.

The primary implementation language is C++. Mathematical concepts also apply to Java and Python, subject to differences in libraries, numeric behavior, recursion, and performance.

Suggested timelines assume roughly 8–12 focused hours per week. They are estimates rather than qualification requirements. Topic completion, problem counts, and platform ratings do not guarantee advancement in an ICPC contest.

Expanded learning content and sources

All 56 modules now include an original explanation, a worked example or reasoning walkthrough, a three-step learning sequence, and annotated learning resources. The original prerequisites, mastery checks, pitfalls, study links, and selected practice problems remain part of the guides.

Resources were researched on 27 October 2026. Links identify YouTube recordings, publisher or curriculum pages, downloadable university slides and handouts, tutorials, reference libraries, and selected research papers. Each module states which part its companion lectures cover. Specialist papers are optional depth after the implementation-level material.

Major collections include MIT OpenCourseWare, Stanford CS97SI, Jeff Erickson's algorithms handouts, Algorithms Live!, USACO Guide, and CP-Algorithms. Sources are linked beside the relevant topic, so no separate bibliography is needed during study.

The resource inventory records topic-to-source mappings. Reading-link checks record HTTP responses; a blocked request or a network failure does not establish that a source is unavailable.
